The right crew.
The right door.

A transparent trailer scheduling experiment: compare balanced door queues with a capacity-aware recommendation using the same synthetic arrivals.

SIMULATED DATA · Educational planning model. No employer systems or operational data are connected.

Shift assumptions

Three doors; up to six productive people at each. Crew assignments stay fixed for this experiment.

Trailer arrivals

Change arrival times or package volume, then recommend assignments again.

What decision does this improve?

A sort supervisor decides which doors to staff, how many people to assign, and which arrived trailer to unload next. The model makes tradeoffs between throughput, arrival timing, belt limits, and assigned labor visible.

Method, baseline, and limitations

The baseline distributes all productive crew evenly and assigns trailers to doors in repeating order. The recommendation enumerates feasible fixed crew assignments and unloads the largest available trailer when a staffed door becomes free. It first maximizes packages completed by the target, then minimizes assigned crew-hours to completion. If no candidate improves the baseline, it retains it.

Simulation uses one-minute steps over 24 hours. Shared belt capacity is split proportionally across active doors. New trailers start on the next minute boundary; unused capacity inside a minute is not reassigned. A zero belt or insufficient productive crew can prevent completion. This is a constrained heuristic, not a globally optimal schedule.

Assigned crew-hours = (assigned unloaders + support) × elapsed hours to completion, including waiting. Unassigned available workers are assumed redeployed. Lower assigned hours are not payroll savings. Breaks, walking, trailer swaps, congestion, safety staffing, package mix, and equipment failure are omitted.

How success is measured

Packages processed before target, finish time, and assigned crew-hours are computed for both plans on the identical input. The source includes capacity and conservation tests. A real pilot would compare predicted vs. actual completion and quality on observed shifts before using recommendations operationally.
