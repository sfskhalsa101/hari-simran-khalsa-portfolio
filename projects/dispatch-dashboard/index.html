A rule-based review queue for dispatchers: identify late arrivals, missing scans, and routes at risk of missing their service window.

SYNTHETIC SNAPSHOT · Five fictional routes. Flags are transparent rules, not AI predictions or live tracking.

Review controls

Changing review time applies rules to the same scan counts and remaining stops. It does not replay historical events. “Mark reviewed” records a session-only acknowledgement and does not resolve the underlying exception.

The operational decision

Which route needs attention first, why is it flagged, and what should the dispatcher verify? This prototype translates dispatch experience into explicit requirements and an auditable review queue.

Rules and success measures

Late arrival: actual or elapsed arrival delay exceeds the tolerance. Missing scans: expected minus scanned packages is positive within 15 minutes of planned departure. Gaps at or after departure are critical. Service risk: estimated finish exceeds the route deadline.

Estimated finish = later of review time, planned departure, and known arrival + remaining stops × minutes per stop + remaining travel minutes. This simple workload estimate excludes traffic, breaks, service-time variance, and contractual details. Critical routes sort before review routes, then clear routes.

The demo measures routes needing intervention, scan gaps, and routes past their estimated service window. A pilot would measure time to acknowledgement, missed exceptions, and false alarms against dispatcher-labelled events. No measured production improvements are claimed.
